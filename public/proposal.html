01 — Executive Summary

Construction labor overruns are most dangerous when they are discovered too late.

McKinsey has reported that 98% of megaprojects experience cost overruns of more than 30%, and 77% are almost behind by half of the planned schedule. Poor communication and inconsistent reporting are listed as major causes. Autodesk and FMI also estimated that bad construction data cost the global construction industry $1.85 trillion in 2020, with 30% of survey respondents saying more than half of their project data was bad.

The difference of catching a productivity drift on week 3 instead of week 12 could mean saving millions of dollars. In an industry where typical contractor pre-tax net margins are often only around 5–6%, even a few points of profit fade can erase the expected return on a job.

Landex Systems replaces manual progress tracking with a 3D model that updates itself. Instead of hand-marked blueprints and delayed field reports, Landex uses spatial Artificial Intelligence (AI) to turn routine site walkthroughs, photos, and checklists into near-instaneous updates, showing exactly what IWPs are installed, what isn't, and how real earned value compares against burn rate, labor budget, and schedule.

Through Landex, you have a visual, component-level understanding of progress, and can receive warnings when earned value is not keeping pace with labor spend or budget thresholds. This allows managers to intervene earlier, protect margin, reduce reporting lag, and prevent small field inefficiencies from turning into large financial overruns, saving millions in downstream losses.

Problem Statement
03 — Problem Statement

P1 Construction's current tracking workflow uses a Kanban board to manage installation tasks and estimate labor productivity based on progress reported during engineer walkthroughs. This gives the team a structured way to organize work, monitor task status, and connect field progress to labor performance.

However, the biggest limitation is the time gap between what happens on-site and when the tracking system is updated.

Today, progress updates depend on a person physically walking the site, observing what has changed, interpreting installation status, and then manually translating those observations into the Kanban board. This process takes time, introduces subjectivity, and creates delay. By the time the board reflects the latest field conditions, the project team may already be working from outdated information.

This creates several operational weaknesses:

Progress updates are not automatic and thus, not timely.
The Kanban board must be manually updated after walkthroughs, which means the system does not continuously reflect the true state of the jobsite.
Field changes are delayed before becoming actionable data.
Installed work, incomplete work, and missed progress may not appear in the tracking system until after the walkthrough is completed and the update is entered.
Labor productivity insights lag behind actual site conditions.
If labor hours are being spent faster than work is being installed, that mismatch may not be visible until after the project has already lost time or budget.

Landex Systems can reduce the time it takes to understand actual site progress, automate part of the reporting workflow, and surface labor-efficiency risks earlier, before small delays become larger budget losses.

Our Solution
04 — Our Solution

Landex Systems provides a full-stack platform augmented with real-time spatial intelligence, that automates the whole site to model translation and metrics calculations commonly used for Earned Value Management (EVM). The proposed workflow operates as follows:

STEP 1
Import the Project Model
P1 provides the project model, drawing set, or BIM file for the scope being tracked, and Landex organizes it into trackable components and areas as the planned state of the finished work. Schedules, budgets, and other reference documents can be uploaded alongside it so progress can be measured against scope, schedule, and labor budget.
STEP 2
Define Installation Work Packages (IWPs)
Landex works with P1 to break the scope into IWPs, each tied to a cost code, at whatever grain P1 wants to monitor, from systems and floors down to specific installed sections or quantities rather than a broad task like "install ductwork." Each IWP carries its planned labor hours, target dates, and responsible team.
STEP 3
Capture Daily Ground-Truth Evidence and produce daily reports
Each day, field teams record progress in the Landex platform by checking off installed components and attaching a photo or video as objective proof of what is actually in place. Landex generates a visual, evidence-backed daily report from the verified model, showing:
  • The updated 3D model reflecting verified installed work
  • Earned value compared against charged labor hours and budget, as a productivity factor per IWP and cost code
  • Warnings and risk signals when burn outpaces verified install

The result is earlier, more objective decision-making, letting P1 catch a falling productivity factor and budget risk while there is still time to act, rather than after the loss is locked in.

Success Metrics
06 — Success Metrics

Under the guidance of Craig Fritts, Director of Advanced Technology at P1 Construction, Landex Systems has developed a working prototype of the proposed construction progress intelligence tool.

The objective of this proposal is to move beyond a controlled prototype environment and apply Landex Systems to a real, large-scale, early-stage construction project for one month without any financial commitment on behalf of P1 Construction. The goal is to evaluate how effectively the tool can capture field progress, identify labor inefficiencies, and provide earlier warning signs when installation progress is not keeping pace with labor spend or planned productivity.

Progress-detection accuracy
Landex's verified percent-complete per IWP matches an independent manual audit within ±10 percentage points on at least 85% of a weekly audited sample of 30+ active IWPs, measured each week of the pilot.
Reporting latency / earlier warning
Landex delivers a verified progress-and-productivity update within one business day of each walkthrough. Establish P1's current Kanban update lag in week one as the baseline, and show the reduction.
Productivity-drift detection
Using Landex-verified earned progress combined with P1 timesheet hours, compute a performance factor per IWP against the planned baseline and flag any IWP deviating by more than 15%. Of the IWPs P1's team independently confirms drifted during the month, Landex flags at least 75%, and surfaces them a median of 3+ days earlier than the current state-of-the-art did, while raising no more than two false flags per week so the signal stays trustworthy.

Resource Requirements
07 — Resource Requirements
1
Access to an Early-Stage Project
Landex requests access to a large-scale project at a stage of construction early enough for progress tracking to yield actionable insight, with active installation underway across multiple zones, floors, or building systems.
2
Project Model, Drawings, or Spatial Documentation
P1 Construction will provide the project BIM model, drawing set, or equivalent spatial documentation required to construct the virtual 3D project environment. This documentation need not be complete at pilot commencement; Landex will establish a baseline model from the available information that represents the planned installation scope and organizes the project into trackable areas and components.
3
Installation Work Package Register and Scope Mapping
P1 Construction will provide the authoritative IWP register or work breakdown structure, together with sufficient information to map model elements and installation scope to their corresponding IWPs, and the planned percent-complete profile for each IWP across the pilot window. All pilot metrics are reported at the IWP level against a planned baseline; this mapping and schedule define the unit of measurement and the expected rate of progress.
4
Planned Labor Budgets and Productivity Benchmarks
P1 Construction will provide planned labor-hour budgets, productivity targets, or industry benchmarks such as MCAA labor units for each scope under evaluation. These figures establish the planned baseline against which verified installation progress and actual labor consumption are compared to identify areas of potential risk.
5
Actual Labor Timesheets at Work Package and Trade Granularity
P1 Construction will provide actual labor hours booked, itemized by IWP and by trade, at the cadence in which they are recorded, weekly or more frequently. This data complements the planned budgets in Requirement 4 and constitutes the actual-hours input to the performance-factor calculation underlying the productivity-drift metric.
6
Field Walkthrough Access
Landex requests permission to conduct, accompany, or receive the outputs of regular site walkthroughs, including photographs, video, checklist responses, field notes, and progress updates recorded by engineering, superintendent, foreman, or other field personnel. These outputs provide the on-site ground truth that Landex maps to the virtual 3D model.
7
Independent Ground-Truth Audit Partner
P1 Construction will designate a field or project-controls representative to conduct a brief weekly audit of a sample of no fewer than 30 active IWPs, recording an independent percent-complete assessment for each, and to confirm at the conclusion of the pilot which IWPs experienced genuine productivity drift. This reference data is the basis against which the progress-detection accuracy and productivity-drift metrics are scored; the estimated commitment is approximately two to three hours per week.
8
Current Tracking Board Export and Update History
P1 Construction will provide an export of the existing Kanban tracking board, including edit timestamps and the complete update history over the pilot period, together with a description of the conditions that currently prompt recognition of a productivity issue. This establishes the baseline for the reporting-latency metric, the comparator for the earlier-warning component of the drift metric, and the basis for quantifying the discrepancy between self-reported and independently verified progress.

Next Steps
08 — Next Steps

Landex Systems is grateful for the opportunity to work with P1 Construction and to develop this prototype under the guidance of Craig Fritts, Director of Advanced Technology.

The proposed one-month pilot is the next step in validating Landex Systems in a real project environment. By applying the tool to an active, large-scale, early-stage construction project, P1 will be able to evaluate whether a daily updated virtual 3D model can add to current PM workflows, provide clearer ground truth from the field, and surface warning signs when labor spend is outpacing verified installation progress.

The goal of this pilot is not to disrupt P1's current process, but to strengthen it. Landex will work alongside the existing workflow by connecting reported task progress to on-site evidence, visual installation status, and budget-risk alerts. This will give P1 a clearer way to see what has been installed, what remains incomplete, and where corrective action may be needed before small inefficiencies snowball into larger project losses.

As a next step, Landex Systems respectfully requests the opportunity to partner with P1 Construction on a one-month pilot project. We would welcome the chance to align on the project scope, identify the necessary data inputs, define success metrics, and begin testing the platform on a real jobsite.

We propose scheduling a kickoff meeting with P1's project and technology stakeholders to select a pilot project, confirm the scope of work, and establish the one-month testing timeline. Landex Systems would be honored to continue working with P1 Construction and demonstrate how objective, 3D ground-truth progress tracking can help protect labor budgets, improve project visibility, and support better field decision-making.
